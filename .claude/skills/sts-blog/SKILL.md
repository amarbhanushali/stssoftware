# Samarth Tech Software blog writing

Adapted from Sentry's blog writing standards for a different reader: **business owners and managers in Nashik and across India** deciding whether, and how, to invest in a website, software, an app or AI automation. Read `CLAUDE.md` first; its rules still apply.

**The bar:** every post should be something a business owner forwards to their partner or manager with "read this before we decide", or something a developer would happily send a client who asks the same question.

## The STS voice

**We sound like:** an experienced developer explaining something over chai to a business owner they respect: practical, specific, honest about costs and trade-offs, never talking down.

**We don't sound like:** a corporate brochure, a press release, a sales deck or an AI-generated summary.

- Use "we" (Samarth Tech Software) and "you" (the reader). It is a conversation.
- Plain English. Explain any technical term in the same sentence, or replace it. "API" becomes "a connection between two systems" unless the reader is a developer.
- Use everyday examples from Indian businesses: Excel registers, WhatsApp orders, Tally exports, GST invoices, dealers, branches, field staff, budget Android phones.
- British spelling, matching the site ("optimise", "organisation").
- Opinions are welcome when they are earned: "Do not start with an app. Start with a website that works on a phone."
- Humour is fine if it helps the point. One light line per post is plenty.

## Banned language

Automatic red flags. Rewrite on sight:

- "We're excited/thrilled to announce", "We are delighted" — just say it
- "Best-in-class", "industry-leading", "cutting-edge", "world-class", "state-of-the-art", "next-gen"
- "Seamless(ly)", "robust", "scalable solution", "end-to-end solution", "one-stop shop"
- "Empower", "leverage", "unlock", "streamline", "elevate", "revolutionise", "supercharge", "harness", "delve", "game-changer"
- "In today's fast-paced digital world", "In the ever-evolving landscape", "digital transformation journey"
- "At Samarth Tech Software, we believe…" — state the belief
- Filler: "That being said", "It's worth noting that", "At the end of the day", "Without further ado", "As you might know", "In conclusion"
- "In this blog post, we will explore…" — start with the point
- Guarantees: "guaranteed results", "100% secure", "rank #1 on Google", "bug-free"

## The opening (first 2–3 sentences)

The intro must **answer the title's question** or **state the problem sharply**. No background, no company history, no hype. The intro is also the page's meta description and the sentence AI assistants quote, so keep it 120–160 characters and make it a complete answer.

**Good:** "When people spend more time fixing and merging copies of the sheet than using it. Size alone is not the reason."

**Bad:** "In today's digital age, businesses are increasingly looking for ways to improve efficiency. In this post, we explore whether software can help."

## Structure: follow the reader's questions

1. **What problem is this, and is it mine?** (one or two short sections)
2. **What actually works, and how?** The bulk of the post. Concrete steps, real examples, what to ask a vendor.
3. **What are the trade-offs or alternatives?** Website vs app, WordPress vs custom, build vs buy, AI vs a simple rule. This is what separates a useful post from a sales page.
4. **What should I do next?** One concrete action the reader can take this week.

For "how we would build it" or technical posts, also cover:
5. **What tends to go wrong?** Common mistakes and how to avoid them.
6. **Where does this approach stop working?** Honest limits.

## Headings must carry information

Every `sections` heading should make sense on its own, because Google shows headings in results and AI tools lift them as answers.

**Weak:** "Introduction", "Background", "Benefits", "Conclusion"

**Strong:** "Watch for the warning signs", "What happens when the connection fails?", "Why a website usually comes before an app"

## Honesty and accuracy

- **Numbers over adjectives, but only real numbers.** Use a figure only if it is verifiable (a cited public source, or data the owner provides). Never invent statistics, percentages, client results or prices. If you have no number, describe the effect concretely instead ("the order goes in once instead of three times").
- **Prices and timelines:** give ranges only when the owner has approved them. Otherwise explain what drives the cost.
- **No invented stories.** Client stories, names and outcomes need the owner's approval and the client's permission. Hypothetical scenarios must read as hypothetical ("Imagine a distributor in Satpur who…").
- **AI claims stay modest:** "suggests", "drafts", "flags for review", never "understands", "decides" or "replaces your team".
- **Other tools and competitors:** it is fine to say WordPress, Zoho or Shopify is the right answer for some readers. That builds trust.

## Titles

The title is the highest-leverage sentence. Write it as the question or claim the reader would type into Google or ask ChatGPT. It must be 60 characters or fewer, as it is followed by "| Samarth Tech Software".

**Strong:**
- "When is it time to replace your Excel sheet with software?"
- "Website or mobile app: which should your business build first?"
- "How much does a business website cost in Nashik?" (only with owner-approved price ranges)

**Weak:**
- "Digital transformation for SMEs"
- "The importance of a website"
- "AI-powered solutions for your business"

## The closing

The article page already ends with "Thinking about something similar for your business?" and a consultation button, so the last section should not repeat a call to action. End on the most useful practical step, warning or decision rule. Never end with a recap or hype ("The future is digital!").

## Post types

| Type | Goal | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decision guide | Help the reader choose between options, with trade-offs | "WordPress or custom website?" |
| Planning guide | Prepare the reader before they hire anyone | "How to plan a software project before you hire a developer" |
| Checklist | A practical list to run before a launch or purchase | "A simple checklist before your website goes live" |
| Explainer | Make one technical idea clear for non-technical readers | "What does 'offline-first' mean for a field app?" |
| Problem guide | Recognise a business problem and the realistic fixes | "Signs your order process needs software" |
| Project story | A real project, with approval from the owner and client | Only with real, approved details |

## The "would they forward it?" test

Before finishing, ask: would a business owner forward this to a colleague, or would a developer send it to a client? If not, it needs more depth or more honesty, or it should not be a post. Posts worth sharing contain at least one of:

- a decision explained with honest trade-offs
- a checklist that saves real time or money
- warning signs the reader can recognise in their own business
- questions to ask a vendor, us included
- a mistake that is common, and how to avoid it

## Non-negotiables

1. Never invent facts: no fake statistics, clients, reviews, prices or staff.
2. Byline: the site shows "Samarth Tech Software team". Use a named author only when the owner gives a real person's name.
3. Never say "we're excited to announce". Just say it.
4. The intro answers the title in 120–160 characters.
5. The title is 60 characters or fewer and phrased the way people search.
6. If you recommend an approach, say what you did not recommend and why.
7. Know who the post is for before writing (owner, manager, developer).
8. No duplicate topics: check existing `articles` in `src/data.js` first and extend a post instead of repeating it.
9. When in doubt, be more specific. A thin post hurts more than a long one.
10. Write the post you would want a client to read before your first meeting.

## Adding the post to the website

Posts live in the `articles` array in `src/data.js`. There is no CMS and no Markdown: each section is a heading plus **one plain-text paragraph** (no links, lists, code or images inside the text).

```js
{
  slug: "website-or-mobile-app-first", // lowercase, hyphenated, stable forever
  category: "Web Development", // reuse an existing category where possible
  services: ["website-development"], // 1–2 related service slugs; links the guide and the service pages both ways
  title: "Website or mobile app: which should your business build first?",
  intro: "For most businesses, a website that works well on a phone comes first. Build an app when customers need it every week, not once.",
  minutes: 4, // about 200 words per minute, rounded up
  sections: [
    ["Heading that answers a question", "One focused paragraph of about 40–100 words."],
    // 4–6 sections
  ],
},
```

- Existing categories: Project Planning, Practical AI, Business Systems, Web Development, Mobile Apps. A new category creates a new filter button, so add one only when several posts will use it.
- Apostrophes: use ’ or plain words inside double-quoted strings, and keep the file valid JavaScript.
- BlogPosting JSON-LD, the sitemap, `llms.txt`, breadcrumbs and the page title are generated automatically.
- If a post needs lists, links or images, propose the data-model change to the owner before doing it. Do not hard-code one-off markup in `App.jsx`.

Then run:

```bash
npm run build && npm test
```

Open `/insights/<slug>` in the browser (`sts-website` launch config) at desktop and phone widths.

## Reviewing or editing a draft

Run all three checklists. When giving feedback, quote the weak passage, explain why it is weak and rewrite it to show the standard.

**Accuracy**
- Every claim is true, and numbers have a source or the owner's approval
- No invented clients, prices, results or staff
- Technical explanations are correct and not oversimplified to the point of being wrong
- AI capabilities are described modestly

**Editorial**
- The intro answers the title within two sentences, in 120–160 characters
- No banned language, filler or brochure tone
- Headings carry information on their own
- Trade-offs or alternatives are covered
- Right length: no padding, not thin

**Search and site fit**
- Title of 60 characters or fewer, phrased as a search query or claim
- Nashik or India mentioned only where it is natural and true
- Not a duplicate of an existing article
- Category reused where possible; build and tests pass
